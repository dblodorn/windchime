# Chime (windchime)

A wind chime simulation with 2–8 tubes (TUBES, 6 by default). Wind pushes a sail, the sail swings a disc clapper, and every strike on a tube is a note. Each unit can instead be a furin bell (INSTRUMENT): one bell, one note (ROOT), its clapper swung by a paper strip in the wind. It plays in the browser (Tone.js + an SF2 sampler) and sends live MIDI to hardware (Web MIDI). SHARE copies a link that carries the whole composition, and MENU opens COMPOSITIONS, a public list of compositions anyone can save anonymously and load (stored by the dmbk GraphQL API). The page holds 1–16 chime units (CHIME 1, CHIME 2, …): COPY on a unit makes another with its state, each unit has its own settings, sound, MIDI channel and audio OUTPUT, and all of them share the MIDI port and the audio device. AUDIO picks the output device (any device the OS exposes, such as a USB class-compliant multichannel interface), and each unit's OUTPUT sends it to a channel pair or a single channel of that device. The page title is "Chime"; there is no on-page heading and no AUDIO or MIDI status line. The engine can still record takes and build `.mid` files (sections 8 and 10), but the UI no longer exposes REC, RENDER or SAVE MIDI, and it has no .sf2 upload: the SOUNDFONT select offers the bundled GM font and the built-in synth voices.

- Live: https://chime.dmbk.io (Vercel project `windchime`, team `db13`; also served at https://windchime-lyart.vercel.app). DNS for dmbk.io is on DigitalOcean: `chime` is a CNAME to the Vercel project target `34679086444df785.vercel-dns-016.com`.

### lib/engine.js sections, in order

1. **Raster**: 200×200 1-bit scratch buffer (`px`, `line`, `present(ch)`), shared: each chime draws into it in turn and presents it to its own canvas in that chime's ink (`setInk(hex)`, its MIDI channel's color; black by default) on white. The figure is still computed in the original 192×232 three-quarter projection and mapped onto the paper by one scale, `toX/toY` with `K = 0.8, TOP = 0`. `seg()` draws a line through that transform, and `outline(cx, cy, rx, ry, n)` is a closed ellipse made of `n` straight `seg`s. K was set from measured extents: the figure fits at rest and in about 99% of frames at WIND 0.45. Stronger gusts clip at the paper edge, by choice; never limit the sim to avoid it.
2. **Pitch and scales**: `noteName`, `mtof`, `cutoffHz(x) = 60·2^(8x)` (slider 0..1 → 60 Hz–15 kHz), `SCALES` (grouped; 32 scales, Ryukyu first, Korea second), `SCALE_LIST`, `tubeNotes(deg, n)` (the first n notes, wrapping around an octave up when the scale is shorter).
3. **Params**: `defaultParams()`: each chime has its own `P` (volume, tubes, wind, decay, cutX, cutoff, q, root, scale, output), changed through its `setParams`. A chime's `noteOf(i) = foldNote(P.root + P.scale[i])` (read modulo the scale's length as a backstop). `P.root` is a MIDI note from 0 to 119 (C-1–B8); `foldNote` lowers a note above 127 by octaves until it fits (B8 plus QUARTAL on 8 tubes reaches 144), so sound, MIDI and recordings stay in range. The tube readout and TEST NOTE use the same `foldNote`; `tubeLen` (the drawing) uses the unfolded offsets, so folded tubes aren't drawn longer. The UI sends `tubes` and the matching `scale` (`tubeNotes(deg, tubes)`) in one `setParams` call.
4. **Geometry + simulation**: `makeSim(seed, n = 6)` and `step(sim, P, dt, onHit)` at a fixed 240 Hz. `step` runs `stepWind` (the wind force `F`, shared by both instruments; its random draws keep their old order, so a tube chime's strikes for a seed are unchanged by the furin) and then `stepTubes` or `stepFurin` by `P.instrument`. Each sim carries its tube layout, `sim.lay = layout(n)`: tube i hangs at `start(n) + i·2π/n` on the ring, where `START` gives each count's start angle (2 → −20°, 3 → −50°, 4 → −38.5°, 5 → −74°, 7 → −58.5°, 8 → −73°), chosen to spread the tubes widest on screen through the YAW view (narrowest gap ≥ 8.9 raster px); 6 keeps the original `−π/2 + π/6`, so its strikes for a given seed are unchanged. `setParams({ tubes })` (clamped to `MIN_TUBES`–`MAX_TUBES`, 2–8) calls `resize`: tubes 1…min(old, new) keep their motion, new ones start at rest, every tube gets one 80 ms cooldown (so a tube moved onto the clapper doesn't strike), and the wind, clapper, sail and random stream carry on. Wind is an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck force with `sqrt(P.wind)` scaling; the clapper is a damped spring; the tubes have their own springs; strikes are impulse collisions with restitution. Velocity is `24 + 1.1·|vr|` (clamped 1..127), with an 80 ms per-tube cooldown. The constants were tuned by measuring strikes per minute (≈1 at wind 0.1, ≈17 at 0.2, ≈57 at 0.45, ≈80 at 0.8, with 6 tubes). Re-measure if you change them. The rate follows the tube count on purpose (seed 12345, WIND 0.45, 5 min): about 29/min at 2 tubes, 34 at 3, 47 at 4, 64 at 5, 67 at 6, 75 at 7 and 92 at 8.
   - **Furin** (`P.instrument === "furin"`): the bell is rigid on its hook; `sim.fu` holds the clapper `c` and the paper strip `q`, two coupled masses in the horizontal plane. The wind drives only the strip, the strip drags the clapper (that lag is the furin's uneven rhythm), and a strike fires where the clapper meets the bell's wall (`FRC`, the wall radius at `CLAP_Y` minus the clapper's), with restitution `FE`. A strike disarms the clapper until it is 1.5 units back off the wall, with a 40 ms minimum gap, so a clapper pressed to the wall doesn't strike every step. Velocity is `12 + 115·(1 − e^(−vr/45))` (a soft curve: hard swings still differ, almost none reach 127). Constants `FA`…`FGAP` were tuned by measuring (5 min, 4 seeds): about 0.3 strikes/min at WIND 0.1, 9 at 0.2, 36 at 0.45, 53 at 0.8; at 0.45 velocities run about 20–122 (10th–90th percentile 48–103) and mean velocity rises with WIND; no strikes in still air; the figure never clipped in 10 min at 0.45. Re-measure if you change them. `setParams({ instrument })` → `switchInstrument`: the incoming instrument starts at rest (a fresh `sim.fu`, or rest tubes with one 80 ms cooldown and the clapper `s` at rest); the wind and `t` carry on. A furin's `noteOf(i)` is `P.root` (`noteAt`); it keeps TUBES and the scale in `P`, unused.
5. **Drawing** (*Design By Numbers* style): single-pixel lines only, no fills, so it's a wireframe with no depth sort.
   - Order: wind dashes, hook, ring `outline` (24 segments), tubes, clapper `outline` (16) and sail rectangle.
   - Tubes: `tubeGeom(P, lay, i, tb)` gives each tube's string attach point, top and bottom; `drawTube` draws the string and an outlined body ±`RT` wide.
   - Camera: every drawn sim position goes through `view(x, z)`, a `YAW = 20°` turn about the vertical axis. Without it, the 6-tube layout's tubes 1/3 and 4/6 share a screen x and merge. The sim itself is not rotated.
   - Wind: each chime's `streaks` (`makeStreaks()`) are solid dashes in projection space, wrapping at the paper edges `OX0`/`OX1`.
   - Furin (`drawFurin`): hook, crown `outline`, the dome's two silhouette edges (8 `seg`s each of `bellR(y)`), the rim `outline` at `BELL_RIM`, the clapper on a string from the crown, and the strip (a rectangle narrowing with the wind angle, like the sail) on a string below the clapper. The bell is axisymmetric, so YAW only moves the clapper and strip. `bellOutline()` gives its silhouette rows on the paper (for tests).
6. **Audio**: per chime, voices → its native input GainNode (target = VOLUME while its SOUND is on, else 0, ramped about 40 ms by `level`) → its `Tone.Filter` low-pass (FREQUENCY = cutoff, RESONANCE = Q, −12 dB/oct) → its router (`route`) → the bus of its channel pair → one `ChannelMerger(N)` → the destination, set to N channels, `explicit`, `discrete` (built by `setupOutput` on the first `startAudio()`; `master` is the merger, only a "started" marker now).
   - **Own context:** `startAudio()` makes the native `AudioContext` itself and hands it to Tone (`Tone.setContext(new Tone.Context(ctx))`), because `setSinkId` lives on the native context. It passes `sinkId` to the constructor only when the UI says the device is listed (`setAudioDevice(id, { listed })`): Chrome breaks the context ("encountered an error from the audio device") on an unknown id instead of throwing. Otherwise it switches with `setSinkId` right after, which fails cleanly.
   - **Buses:** one per pair k (channels 2k+1, 2k+2), made on first use: native `DynamicsCompressor` (threshold −16, ratio 4, attack 3 ms, release 250 ms, knee 30 = the old `Tone.Compressor`) → Gain 0.85 → `ChannelSplitter(2)` → merger inputs 2k, 2k+1. Per pair because a compressor holds at most 2 channels and a shared detector would duck one output when another hits. With N = 2 the output is bit-identical to the old single chain (rendered and compared). N is `maxChannelCount`, capped at 32 (`MAX_OUT`); a device change (`setSinkId`, `sinkchange`) rebuilds the merger and re-routes every chime (`refreshOutput`).
   - **Routing:** a chime's `P.output` is `{ first, count }` (`outputOf("3-4")` → `{ first: 2, count: 2 }`). A pair connects the filter straight to its bus; one channel goes through a mono GainNode (`channelCount 1`, `speakers` fold) into one input of a per-chime `ChannelMerger(2)` and then that pair's bus. Units on the same or overlapping outputs are summed there and share that bus's compressor. When the device lacks a chime's channels it is routed to 1–2 (`outputAvailable()` false) and goes back when they return. Changing OUTPUT reconnects only the router, so ringing notes move without being cut. `onOutputChange(fn)` fires with N when audio starts and whenever N changes; `outputChannels()` is null before audio starts; `audioOutSupported()` is `"setSinkId" in AudioContext.prototype` (Chromium only).
   - **Voices:** a chime's graph (`buildGraph`) is made when its SOUND first turns on; its FM `PolySynth` only when it plays FM CRYSTAL. `ring(ch, …)` schedules nothing for a silent chime (SOUND off, VOLUME 0, or `setMuted`). Built-in `VOICES` are additive partials (tubular bells 1/2.756/5.404/8.933, etc.) or the FM synth, each mapped to a GM program for MIDI. `VOICES[6]` is FURIN (bell-plate partials 1/2.405/4.12/5.94/8.47, an octave up, with the chorus beat; GM Tinkle Bell, 112), appended at the end like everything indexed. `setMuted` silences every chime's browser sound only.
7. **SF2 sampler**: `parseSF2` (RIFF walk, phdr/pbag/pgen/inst/ibag/igen/shdr), `resolvePreset` (merges global and local zones; key/vel ranges, root override, coarse/fine tune, attenuation, pan, loop mode), `sampleBuffer` (lazy Int16 → AudioBuffer, cached per font), `playSF`. SF2 volume envelopes are ignored on purpose: DECAY sets every note's length. Attenuation uses the EMU/FluidSynth 0.4 factor.
   - **Loading in pieces**: `loadFont(url, name, { signal })` allocates one buffer of the file's full size and fills it with ranged fetches, so `parseSF2` and `sampleBuffer` read their usual offsets. On load it fetches `bytes=0-4095` (total size from `Content-Range`, RIFF walk to the `pdta` list) and then `pdta` to the end: 2 requests, about 209 KB of the 6 MB. Samples come later through `loadSamples(regions)`: it skips samples in `f.loaded` or `f.pending`, merges byte ranges closer than 16 KB into one request (Tubular Bells is 1 request of 71 KB, the Standard drum kit 3, no preset more than 7), and forgets a failed download so the next call retries; it never rejects. A `200` answer (a server ignoring Range) is the whole file: `f.all` is set and nothing more is fetched. `addFont(arrayBuffer)` (Node, tests) also sets `f.all`.
   - **When samples load**: only `setSound(true)` and `chooseVoice` on a sounding chime call `loadSamples`, so a muted unit, or MIDI-only use, downloads no samples. Until a note's samples are in, `playSF` plays that note with `VOICES[0]` (built-in tubular bells) through `playVoice`, the built-in path factored out of `ring`, into the same input (VOLUME and filter apply). The check is per note.
8. **Chimes**: `createChime({ seed })` returns one chime: `P`, `noteOf`, `setParams`, `chooseVoice` (returns false and keeps the voice for an unknown preset), `midiProgram`, `setSound(on, { quiet })` (needs `startAudio()` first; plays a short arpeggio on tubes 1, 3 and 5, clamped to the last tube, unless `quiet`), `onHit(fn)` (listeners get `(tubeIndex, velocity)`), `attach`, `tubeSegments`, `strikeAt`, `strikeTube`, `startRec`/`stopRec`/`recording`, `renderTake(sec)` (offline sim with a fresh seed, the chime's tube count and 2 s settle) and `destroy()`. On a furin, `strikeAt` hits inside the bell's silhouette padded 6 px (returns 0, else −1), `tubeSegments()` is `[]`, `strikeTube(0)` kicks the clapper off the wall (and blocks a follow-on strike for 250 ms), and SOUND on plays one strike instead of the arpeggio.
9. **Loop**: a chime's `attach(canvas)` adds it to the `live` set; one shared requestAnimationFrame `tick` steps each live chime at 240 Hz (its own accumulator), moves its streaks and draws it, and stops when the set is empty. `attach` returns the detach. `strikeAt(x, y)` takes paper pixel coordinates (0–200). It strikes the nearest tube within 6 px of its centerline and its height ±6 and returns its index (−1 for empty paper), through `strikeTube(i)`, which the readout's note pads call directly. `tubeSegments()` returns each tube's centerline on the paper (handy for tests). The canvas is `box-content` with a 1px border outside the drawing, so `ChimeUnit` subtracts the 2 px border from the available width, sizes the drawing area to whole multiples of 200 where it fits (capped at 298 px while `matchMedia("(width < 40rem)")` matches, the same query as Tailwind's `max-sm:`, so a unit is 300 px wide on phones and narrow windows; the `motion.section` carries `max-sm:w-[min(300px,100%)]` to match), sets the readout width to canvas + 2, and maps taps through `clientLeft`/`clientWidth`.
10. **MIDI file**: `buildMidi(take)` → format 0, 480 PPQ, 120 BPM; bank select + program; CC74/CC71 from the take's `cc` list; note-offs after DECAY (cut short by a re-strike of the same note). Drum kits (bank 128) go on channel 10.

### Voice ids

A chime's `chooseVoice(value)`: `"v<i>"` is a built-in voice, `"s<font>:<preset>"` is a preset in `fonts[font]`. The bundled GM font is the only font the UI loads (there is no .sf2 upload), so every `s` id is `s0:<preset>`. A unit's stored `voice` is `null` for the default: GM tubular bells once the font's tables have loaded (its samples load when the unit's SOUND goes on), the built-in tubular bells before. A stored `s` id the font doesn't have goes back to `null` when the font loads. `addFont` still `unshift`s a GM font to index 0, so if uploads ever come back, existing `s` ids need remapping by +1 when the GM font arrives.

## Constraints and decisions to keep

- **Visual language**: one ink on white. The ink is pure black everywhere except inside a chime unit, which draws in its MIDI channel's ink. Pixelated canvas scaled by whole multiples, Knif Mono for every label. Parameter rows are `LABEL ....... value` with dotted leaders. No rounded corners, no shadows.
  - **Channel inks** (`lib/colors.js`, like patch-cable colors): channel 1 is black, 2–16 are Sanzo Wada colors from https://sanzo-wada.dmbk.io (slugs kept in `CHANNEL_INKS`), each ≥ 3:1 against white and ≥ 14.6 CIEDE2000 from every other, chosen as primaries first (CARMINE, BLUE, KHAKI, DIAMINE GREEN, ORANGE…). The ink is derived from `channel`, never stored, so links, saves and the API don't change. `ChimeUnit` spreads `C.inkStyle(hex)` on its `motion.section`; it redefines every black theme token (`--foreground`, `--border`, `--accent`, …) plus `color`, so the drawing's border, round buttons, drawer, flash, leaders and focus outline all follow. `inkStyle` is `undefined` for black, so a channel 1 unit is unchanged. **Popups portal out of the unit** and don't inherit it: `PopoverContent` gets `style={ink}`, `ParamSelect` reads `InkContext` (provided inside the popover) for its `SelectContent`, and `SearchSelect` takes `contentStyle`. Anything new that portals out of a unit must carry the ink too, or it draws black. The page's own controls (button row, MENU, COMPOSITIONS, MIDI/AUDIO dialogs) stay black. The two filled-ink exceptions outside the units are the MIDI OUT legend's swatches and the active-channels strip's squares (see **Channel focus**).
  - It is enforced through the theme: every shadcn color token in `app/globals.css` is `#000000` or `#ffffff` (a unit overrides the black ones with its ink), `--accent` is black (so highlights invert), `--radius` and every `--radius-*` are 0, the `--shadow-*` namespaces are cleared, and there is no `.dark` block or `dark` variant.
  - Fonts: `next/font/local` loads Knif Mono from `app/fonts/` as `--font-ui`; `--font-sans`/`--font-mono`/`--font-heading` point at it. To try another face, change the `src` in `app/layout.jsx`. Don't rename the variable to `--font-mono`, which would refer to itself in `@theme inline`. Knif Mono has one weight, so `font-bold`/`<b>` is synthesized by the browser. Sizes: body 13px, labels and headings 11px, note pads 13px names over 10px Hz.
  - Components use text glyphs (`▾`, `▴`, `■`) instead of icons. The exceptions, by request, are the four 30 px round buttons on each unit's canvas, all Lucide icons (`lucide-react`) with `shapeRendering="crispEdges"` so they stay two-color: COPY in the top-left corner (`CopyPlus`, `aria-label="COPY"`, disabled at 16 units), SOUND in the top-right corner (`Volume2`/`VolumeX`, `aria-label="SOUND"`, `aria-pressed`), SETTINGS in the bottom-right corner (`Settings2`, `aria-label="SETTINGS"`, inverted while open), which opens the parameter popover, and NOTES in the bottom-left corner (`EighthNote`, `aria-label="NOTES"`, `aria-pressed`), which shows the tube-note readout. They are also the only rounded elements (`rounded-[50%]`, which the zeroed `--radius-*` tokens don't touch). One more icon, by request: MENU (`Menu`, square, `h-11 w-11`, `crispEdges`, see **COMPOSITIONS panel**). Don't add other icons or rounded shapes without asking. Focus is `focus-visible:outline-1 outline-dashed outline-foreground outline-offset-3` (no rings, which are box shadows).
  - Adding a component: `pnpm dlx shadcn@4.23.0 add <name>`, then strip `rounded-*`, `shadow-*`, `ring-*`, `dark:*` and lucide icons, and use the dashed focus outline. `lucide-react` is a dependency now (for the four round buttons' icons only); don't use the shadcn CLI's icon imports. The CLI offers to overwrite `button.jsx`: answer no. Overlays get no tint: `dialog.jsx`'s overlay is transparent (it still catches outside clicks), since a grey backdrop would break the two-color rule.
- **Radix specifics in `Chime.jsx`**:
  - Select values must be non-empty strings: numbers go through `String(n)`, and MIDI OUT's NONE is the `"none"` sentinel.
  - `ParamSelect` passes the current choice's text as `SelectValue` children (`display`), because Radix only fills it on the client. `Slider` draws a stand-in thumb until mount for the same reason.
  - `Param` is a `<div>`; controls are named with `aria-labelledby="${id}-label"` (a `<label>` would open selects on click and include the value text in the name).
  - **Units, layout and dragging:** `Chime` holds `units`, an array of plain records `{ id, instrument, volume, tubes, wind, decay, cutX, q, root, scale (by name), voice, channel, midiMode, output, notes, sound, x, y, z }` (plus a transient `soundQuiet`, see **COMPOSITIONS panel**), changed through `change(id, patch)`. CHIME n is the array index + 1. `<main>` is `fixed inset-0` with 16 px (or safe-area) padding; inside it an `isolate` grid puts every unit in the same centered cell (`[grid-area:1/1]`), so a unit's `x`/`y` (motion values) are offsets from the center. `isolate` keeps the units' `zIndex` (their `z`) below the fixed button row. Each unit is a `motion.section` labelled "CHIME n" (also its `stageRef`, which sizes its canvas) that drags with `dragListener={false}`: the canvas's pointerdown calls `dragControls.start(e)` only when `strikeAt` returns −1, so tubes still strike. `dragConstraints={mainRef}`, no elastic, no momentum; motion keeps it inside on resize. Drag end writes `x`/`y` into the record; the record's position is clamped into the viewport once the canvas has its size (`fitted`). A capture-phase pointerdown brings a unit to the front (`z` = max + 1, renumbered 1…n past 1000). COPY clones the record with a new id, the lowest free channel, the source's current position + 24 px and the top `z`; at most 16 units (`MAX_UNITS`). REMOVE (in the popover while more than one unit exists) ends that channel's notes and drops the record. RESET (see **SHARE button**) replaces every unit with `defaultUnit(newId())` through `replaceComposition`: one muted CHIME 1 at the center on channel 1 and OUTPUT 1–2, old notes ended, the audio device kept, no confirmation. The fresh id matters: reusing an old id would keep that unit's engine instance. Units overlap freely, so a copy covers part of its source: tests bring a unit to the front by pressing an uncovered point on its canvas edge before using its buttons. The canvas is `touch-none`. Element ids are prefixed with the unit id (`${id}-p-wind`, `${id}-notes`). The old `html` padding is still there but no longer places anything.
  - **SETTINGS popover:** each unit's fourteen parameter rows (INSTRUMENT, VOLUME, TUBES, WIND, DECAY, FREQUENCY, RESONANCE, ROOT, OCTAVE, SCALE, SOUNDFONT, OUTPUT, CHANNEL, MIDI MODE), then REMOVE while more than one unit exists, live in an uncontrolled, non-modal `Popover` named "SETTINGS CHIME n" (`role=dialog`). The values live in the unit record, so they survive the popover closing (Radix unmounts closed content). Opening one unit's popover closes another's (the outside press dismisses it). The page stays in the accessibility tree while it's open, but its controls only exist while it's open: tests must click SETTINGS before driving a parameter by role, and close it (Escape) before tapping a canvas, which it can cover. The drawing is capped at 2× (400 px). TUBES is a stepped slider: `ParamSlider` with `step={1}` (Radix snaps the thumb) and the `ticks` prop of `components/ui/slider.jsx`, which draws a 1×3 px mark 10 px below the track at every value, placed with the same thumb-inset formula as the SSR stand-in thumb. ROOT and OCTAVE split the unit's one `root` (a MIDI note, 0–119): ROOT is a 12-item `ParamSelect` of `E.NOTE_NAMES` (values are pitch classes `"0"`…`"11"`, no octave, no Hz; its row value is the full note, `C5`), OCTAVE a stepped `ParamSlider` −1…8 with `ticks` (octave 4 holds middle C, so octave = `floor(root / 12) − 1`); each writes `root = 12·(octave + 1) + note`, keeping the other. MIDI MODE is a two-option `ParamSelect`, `"poly"`/`"mono"`. OUTPUT is a `ParamSelect` of the pairs, then the single channels, up to `outChannels` (stored as `"3-4"`/`"5"`, shown with an en dash); a stored value the device lacks is shown as a disabled `UNAVAILABLE` item so `SelectValue` still has it. Selected options carry a `■` mark in their text: strip it when tests compare option names (not for SCALE and SOUNDFONT, see below). The null voice follows the instrument: `defaultVoice(instrument)` is `v6` (FURIN) for a furin, GM tubular bells (or `v0`) for a tube chime, and the effective voice is `unit.voice || defaultVoice(unit.instrument)` everywhere (SOUNDFONT display, `chooseVoice`, program change).

- **SSR**: `Chime.jsx` is `"use client"` but still prerenders. `lib/engine.js` and `lib/midiout.js` must not touch `window`/`document`/`navigator` at module top level; Tone is imported dynamically. Never read a browser capability during render (for example `disabled={!M.supported()}`): the server renders `false`, and React does not fix mismatched attributes during hydration, so the stale value sticks. Hold it in state that starts `null` and is set in a mount effect (see `midiSupported`).

On load each record is checked: unknown scale names fall back to RYUKYU, numbers are clamped to their ranges (`root` to 0–119, default 72 = C5), channel to 1–16, `tubes` rounded into 2–8 (6 when missing), `midiMode` anything but `"mono"` → `"poly"`, `instrument` anything but `"furin"` → `"chime"`, `output` anything but a valid pair (`"1-2"`, `"3-4"`, … up to 32) or channel (`"1"`…`"32"`) → `"1-2"`, more than 16 records are cut, an unreadable value gives one default unit. Those field rules live only in `Sh.UnitSchema` (each field `.catch`es its default on its own); `readUnit` adds the id checks and `defaultUnit` is `{ id, ...Sh.DEFAULTS }`. Change a range or default there. With no `windchime.units`, CHIME 1 takes the single-unit keys `windchime.pos` and `windchime.midiCh`, which are removed after the first save. The server renders one default unit; the stored list replaces it in a mount effect.
- **Share links**: `#c=<base64url>` in the fragment (never sent to the server). The mount effect reads it first: a composition that decodes replaces the saved units (the save effect then writes it over `windchime.units`), anything else is ignored, and either way `history.replaceState` clears the fragment. A `hashchange` listener does the same for a link pasted into an open tab (and ends the old units' MIDI notes). SOUND, OUTPUT, the audio device, the MIDI port and the SEND options are never carried (`pack`/`toSaved` don't read `output`; `unpack`/`fromSaved` leave it to the schema's `"1-2"`), so linked and loaded units are on 1–2 and changing OUTPUT never changes a link. Version 1 bytes: version, unit count (1–16), then 19 bytes per unit in page order: VOLUME×100, TUBES, WIND×100, DECAY×10, FREQUENCY×1000 (u16), RESONANCE×10, ROOT (0–119; it was 48–84 before OCTAVE, so older links decode unchanged), SCALE index, voice (u16: 0 default, 1 + i built-in `v<i>`, 1000 + p GM preset `s0:<p>`), flags (bits 0–3 channel−1, 4 MONO, 5 NOTES), x and y (i16), stacking rank, instrument (0 tube chime, 1 furin; zero in older links, so they open as tube chimes and tube-chime links are unchanged), one reserved zero byte. 16 units make a 433-character link. `encode` checks every packed unit with the strict `PackedUnitSchema` (throws rather than wrap a byte); `decode` returns `null` for bad base64, an unknown version or a wrong length, and runs the result through `CompositionSchema`. **Index rule:** scales and presets travel as indices, so `SCALES` and the bundled `.sf2` may only grow at the end. Reordering, removing or replacing the font needs a version 2 reader in `readers` that maps version 1 indices. After a scale or preset is added, run `pnpm catalog` and copy its output to dmbk-graphql-api's `src/lib/chime/catalog.json` (then its `npm run chime:collection -- --apply`, and deploy it): the API refuses saves with scales or voices it doesn't know.
- **Saved compositions** (dmbk-graphql-api, `openspec/specs/chime-compositions` there): `dmbkSoundChimeCompositions` lists them, `dmbkSoundSaveChimeComposition` saves one anonymously and the API generates its title (ensemble, key, instrument), so the app never sends text. Units go over the wire as the share link's integers under readable names (`toSaved`/`fromSaved` in `lib/share.js`, sharing `pack`/`unpack`, so a loaded composition gives the same SHARE link it was saved from); `fromSaved` is strict (unknown scale, out of range, 0 or more than 16 units → `null`) and ends in `CompositionSchema`. The browser posts only `{ id, variables }` to `app/api/graphql/route.js`, which accepts same-origin POSTs (`Sec-Fetch-Site`, else `Origin` vs `Host`) for ids in the persisted map, and forwards them to `DMBK_GRAPHQL_API_URL` (default `https://graphql.dmbk.network/graphql`) with `x-api-key: DMBK_GRAPHQL_API_KEY` and `x-end-user-ip` (first `x-forwarded-for` entry; the API's per-visitor save limit), nothing else. The key is set in Vercel (Production and Preview); without it saves fail with FORBIDDEN, and in production the route throws. Locally, set both in `.env.local` (git-ignored) to save; reads work keyless. Each unit carries `instrument` (`CHIME`/`FURIN`, enum `ChimeInstrument`; optional on input, and saves made before it come back as `CHIME`), so `fromSaved` reads a missing one as CHIME. The API keeps tube-chime fingerprints unchanged by hashing `instrument` only for non-chime units. Its `root` range (`INT_FIELDS.root` in its `src/lib/chime/shape.ts`, and the collection validator) is 0–119, the same as `UnitSchema`'s; widen both together.
- **MIDI port lifecycle** lives in `lib/midiout.js`, not React. `wanted` is the chosen (or remembered, `windchime.midiOutName`) port *name*; it survives unplugging. `adopt()` runs after `connect()` and on every `statechange`: it selects the connected port named `wanted`, or on connect the only port there is. `setLive(port)` is the only writer of `output`; it calls `port.open()` and bumps `linkSeq`. On disconnect the note-off timers are dropped without sending. Every message takes its channel (1–16) first: `note(ch, n, vel, s, src, mono)`, `testNote(ch, n)`, `program(ch, bank, prog)`, `cc(ch, num, v)`; note-off timers are keyed `channel:note`, one entry `{ timer, end, srcs }` per sounding key. `src` is the striking tube (`${unitId}:${i}`), `mono` the unit's MIDI MODE. A strike on a sounding key from the same `src`, or any strike in MONO, sends the note-off first (one note per key); in POLY (default) a strike from another source stacks a second note-on and the key's single note-off moves out to the later end, so tubes or units sharing a pitch ring together. TEST NOTE passes no `src`, so test notes cut each other as before. `endNotes(ch)` ends one channel's notes (a unit changing channel or being removed; it also ends another unit's notes on the same channel). `allNotesOff(channels)` ends every note, then sends CC123/CC120 on each listed channel. `status()` returns one snapshot for the UI; each `ChimeUnit` keys its program-change/CC effects on `status().linkSeq` and its own voice, filter and channel, so a port that becomes live (chosen, auto-picked, replugged with the same id) gets the setup exactly once. A failed `requestMIDIAccess` is not cached, so CONNECT MIDI can be retried.
- **Strict mode** is on: effects run twice in dev. Engine listeners return unsubscribe functions; keep it that way.

## Commands

```bash
pnpm install
pnpm dev        # http://localhost:3000
pnpm build
pnpm start      # serve the production build
pnpm relay      # Relay compiler (dev and build run it first); writes __generated__/ and the persisted map
pnpm schema:pull  # refresh schemas/dmbk-graphql-api.graphql from the API, then commit it
```

There are no `lint` or `test` scripts. The package manager is pnpm: `pnpm-lock.yaml` is committed and Vercel installs from it with a frozen lockfile, so update it (`pnpm install`) whenever `package.json` changes or the deploy fails. Don't add a `package-lock.json` alongside it.

## Testing

No automated tests yet. What has worked so far:

- **Engine in Node**: `lib/engine.js` imports cleanly in Node (no DOM at top level). You can `addFont` the `.sf2` from `public/` (or test `loadFont`/`loadSamples` with a stubbed global `fetch` that honours `Range`), make a chime with `createChime({ seed })`, and call its `chooseVoice`, `setParams` and `renderTake(60)`, then `buildMidi`, without a browser. Smoke test: `node -e 'import("./lib/engine.js").then(m => console.log(m.SCALE_LIST.length, typeof m.createChime))'` (prints `32 function`). Node warns that the module type isn't specified, because `package.json` has no `"type": "module"`. That's harmless. Don't add the field without checking `next build`.
- **UI in headless Chromium**: Playwright is fine for this. Stub `navigator.requestMIDIAccess` with fake outputs that record `send()` calls, and check the bytes (`0x9n`/`0x8n` on the chosen channel, `0xBn 74/71`, `0xBn 123` on PANIC). Drive controls by role and name (`getByRole("combobox" | "slider" | "checkbox", { name })`); native `selectOption` and input value setters no longer apply. Radix moves select focus on the next tick, so space keystrokes ~50 ms apart. SCALE and SOUNDFONT are typed into (see **SCALE and SOUNDFONT**). Don't run `next dev` while `next start` serves the same `.next` directory: dev overwrites the production build output.
- **COMPOSITIONS in Playwright**: intercept `**/api/graphql` with `context.route` and answer by operation name, looked up from `lib/server/persisted-operations.json` by the posted id. Find the panel by `getByRole("dialog", { name: "COMPOSITIONS" })`; while it is open the units are hidden from the accessibility tree, so check them by DOM (`section[aria-label='CHIME 1']`). The route itself is tested against `pnpm start` with `DMBK_GRAPHQL_API_URL` pointing at a stub server.
- **Audio output in Playwright**: headless Chromium has one stereo device, so fake one in an init script: override `AudioDestinationNode.prototype.maxChannelCount` (and clamp its `channelCount` setter to the real max), stub `AudioContext.prototype.setSinkId` to change the fake count and dispatch `sinkchange`, wrap `AudioContext` to accept the `sinkId` constructor option, and replace `navigator.mediaDevices` with an `EventTarget` whose `enumerateDevices` and `getUserMedia` you control. To hear routing, wrap `AudioNode.prototype.connect` so the merger that feeds the destination also feeds a splitter and one `AnalyserNode` per channel, then read each channel's peak after a strike. Launch with `--mute-audio`. Stereo equivalence of a chain change: render both with an `OfflineAudioContext` and compare samples.
- Things only a person can check: real audio output, a real multichannel interface (device choice, separate outputs, unplug/replug), Safari and Firefox (no `setSinkId` on `AudioContext`: system output only; whether they expose more than 2 channels is unverified), iOS behavior, real MIDI hardware.

Good first additions would be Vitest unit tests for `parseSF2`/`resolvePreset`, `buildMidi` byte output, `tubeNotes`, the POLY/MONO `note()` rule, the share-link round trip (`encode`/`decode`, a pinned version 1 link), and strike-rate regression for the sim.

## Deploying

Pushing to `main` deploys production on Vercel (project `windchime`, team `db13`). Other branches get preview deployments behind Vercel login (standard deployment protection). Static files in `public/` are served as-is; `next.config.mjs` sets long caching and an octet-stream type for `.sf2`. The soundfont is fetched with Range requests (Vercel and `next start` both answer `206`), cached `immutable`, and links store preset indices, so a different font must get a new filename, never replace `TimGM6mb.sf2` in place. `Chime`'s font effect aborts its downloads on `pagehide` and unmount and doesn't log the abort, so a reload during a download is not a console error.
